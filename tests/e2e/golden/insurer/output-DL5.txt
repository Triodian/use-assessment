## What this assessment is, and is not, in ISO terms

In ISO/IEC 42001:2023 terms this assessment is evidence for the supplier control (A.10.3) and, where a provider’s AI is concerned, for operation and monitoring (A.6.2.6), event logs (A.6.2.8) and the communication of incidents (A.8.4). It is not an AI system impact assessment (clause 6.1.4 and A.5; ISO/IEC 42005:2025). The register can be used to choose which systems need one: those at a high authority tier or consequence level.

The assessment does not assess likelihood, bias, data or model behaviour. It grades how far the obtainable evidence falls short of what is expected; that grade is not a risk rating.

## The scales beside ISO vocabulary

### Authority tiers A1 to A6 beside ISO/IEC 22989:2022 and ISO/IEC TS 8200:2024

ISO/IEC 22989:2022 (clause 5.13) describes levels of automation of a system's task, from no automation to autonomy. ISO/IEC TS 8200:2024 describes controllability: whether, when and how a person or another system can intervene. The authority tiers describe something narrower: what the AI's output is allowed to do in the business before a person acts.

| Tier | Here | Nearest level in ISO/IEC 22989:2022, 5.13 | The controllability question (ISO/IEC TS 8200:2024) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A1 Assists | Drafts, summarises, searches. A person still acts | Assistance: the system assists an operator | A person controls every action. Nothing is handed to the system |
| A2 Recommends | Scores, ranks, recommends. A person decides | Assistance to partial automation: some sub-functions are automated and the operator stays in control | Can the person accept, change or reject each output before it takes effect? |
| A3 Decides | Approves, declines, triages. No person reviews before it takes effect | Conditional automation: sustained performance by the system, with a person ready to take over | Can a person intervene before or after an outcome takes effect, and how quickly? |
| A4 Moves money | Prices, allocates, pays | High automation: the system performs parts of its task without intervention | Are there limits on amount and number, and a way to stop or reverse? |
| A5 Acts unattended | Plans and acts across steps and tools, unattended | Full automation: the system performs its whole task without intervention | Can the system be interrupted or constrained while it acts, and by whom? |
| A6 Changes systems | Holds credentials that change systems, assets or infrastructure | Full automation, with authority over other systems. ISO/IEC 22989 keeps the word autonomy for a system that can change its own goals or scope without outside control, which no tier assumes | Can control be taken back, credentials withdrawn and changes rolled back? |

The cross-walk gives the nearest term in each standard so that the two vocabularies can be read side by side. It is Triodian's reading, for the engagement's independent reviewer or another person with the relevant ISO competence to check, and it is not an equivalence. ### Evidence rungs E0 to E6 beside ISO/IEC 27002:2022 supplier controls, ISO/IEC 27036-2:2022 and ISO/IEC 12792:2025

ISO/IEC 27002:2022 controls 5.19 to 5.23 cover supplier relationships, supplier agreements, the ICT supply chain, the monitoring and review of supplier services, and cloud services. ISO/IEC 27036-2:2022 sets requirements for the acquirer and the supplier across the relationship. The ladder is the evidence side of both: what the insurer can hold to show that a provider's AI stayed within the appetite. ISO/IEC 42001:2023 control A.10.3 (suppliers) is the control the ladder serves in an AI management system.

| Rung | Here | ISO/IEC 27002:2022 control it supports | In ISO/IEC 42001:2023 | Note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E0 None | No evidence is held | None | None | A gap against 5.19 where the provider is material |
| E1 Disclosure | A statement of what AI is used, where and for what | 5.19 Information security in supplier relationships; 5.21 Managing information security in the ICT supply chain | A.10.3; A.8.2 as the provider's information for users | A disclosure worth holding names: the system and what it is for; the provider and any party behind it; where it is established and where it runs; what it decides or does, and what a person does; the data it uses; its known limits; and how a change is notified.
